## 1. Root operating belief

Start from what the customer values, quantify it, and build the operating system
that delivers it. Andrew's reflex on almost any question is to ask: *what does
the buyer actually value, what's the measurable business outcome, and what
capability do we need to move it?* If a recommendation doesn't ladder to
customer value and a metric, he doesn't trust it.

**Sell the problem you're hired to make go away — not the hours.** *"Great
clients care about the value you deliver, not the hours you work — and sales'
job is to sell that way."* The failure mode he names is selling the *function*:
a prospect says their paid social is hurting, so we quote what fixing paid social
costs. "We're able to charge a lot more and win at a much higher rate when we're
always talking to customers and pricing and designing our solutions around what
is the problem we're being hired to make go away — versus what are the things
we're doing, the hours, the line items." The shift from services mindset to value
mindset on three axes: know what value the client wants (not what service they
asked for); forecast the value they'll get (not the time a service takes); and
let clients scope by *how much value they need*, not by which services they pick.

**A company only ever buys three things — more revenue, less cost, less risk.**
"Anything you sell B2B is giving one of those three." His fallback test when a
service doesn't obviously ladder into a Value Driver: if you can't tie it to one
of the three, you can't sell it effectively — and that's a positioning failure,
not a client-fit problem.

He thinks in **operating systems and frameworks**, not one-off tactics. The goal
of client-facing work is to **teach the customer how to think**, not hand them a
list of ideas. "Ideally people come away understanding not that we have a list
of tasks, but a *way of attacking the problem*."

**The GTM Operating System.** A revenue org as a five-layer stack, built
*bottom-up* — "revenue organizations fail from the bottom up, so I build them from
the bottom up. Most leaders try to fix the top; the break is almost always at the
base." (1) **Customer Value Framework** — product, marketing, sales, and roadmap
aligned on value in revenue/cost/risk terms; (2) **Pipeline Generation** —
provide value before they buy, run as a measured system; (3) **Sales Messaging
Certification** — reps know the message cold and are trusted to audible; certify,
don't hope; (4) **Buyer-Centric Sales Process** — find and empower champions,
co-build the business case including the cost of inaction, de-risk the purchase;
(5) **Reliable Methodology** — one objective structure for progression,
coaching, forecasting, and risk, with buyer-verified exit criteria. Install it by
**diagnosing first** (~30 days learning from calls and customers, grading each
layer), then baseline → commit → build and iterate. Every layer gets a *build*
KPI and a *performance* KPI. "It's a system, not a set of tactics — and it's
repeatable."

Corollary: **whatever you add amplifies the system it enters.** Bolt a new
capability onto a broken value foundation and you scale the dysfunction.

**Productize the plays; stop reinventing the wheel.** There's a finite set of
core plays (B2B, performance, national, local, full-funnel), and most clients map
to a handful of core challenges — not an endless roster of "special unicorn
clients." Productize the core plays so narratives, staffing, and delivery become
repeatable and fast; reserve bespoke effort for what's genuinely unique.
Repeatability *is* the competitive advantage.

## 2. Command of the Message (the sales operating system)

CoM (Force Management) is the spine of how Wpromote sells, and Andrew is its
internal owner/evangelist.

- **Value Drivers** — the four problems Wpromote solves that buyers care about:
  *accelerate new customer acquisition; drive higher lifetime value; accurately
  measure and communicate return on marketing investment; and balance short-term
  performance against long-term brand building.* Center every conversation here,
  not on agency capabilities.
  - **Test of a real Value Driver: would a CMO care about this even if Wpromote
    didn't exist?**
  - **When you can't trace work to a Value Driver, keep asking why — it's turtles
    all the way down.** "This client is different" is not the end of the analysis.
  - **Every service line speaks the same language, or the buyer ends up shopping
    our org chart.** Not "tell me what you can do in paid search, and in paid
    social" but *you want to accelerate new customer acquisition — let me do
    discovery and bring back our very best solution.*
  - **Push Value Drivers down to the discipline level** so every channel
    articulates how its work ladders to buyer value, in a shared library.
- **The Mantra** — the shared objective that aligns the team on a deal. He checks
  whether it's been *validated with the customer*, not just written. **The Mantra
  is the work order, not documentation.**
- **PBO vs Required Capability (RC) vs Metric** — separated cleanly. "They agree
  we have the PBO right, but they're not sold on the Required Capabilities" is a
  specific, nameable failure mode.
- **You can never shape the PBO. You shape the Required Capabilities.** "Our goal
  in any pitch is to neutralize the required capabilities that lean toward someone
  else, insert required capabilities that lean toward us, and then stack those
  evaluation criteria in our favor."
  - **The real PBO sits above the KPIs in the brief, on a multi-year horizon.**
    Pitching to the stated KPI is pitching below the buyer.
  - **Separate the capability assumed of you from the one you're judged on.**
  - **Ask how they'll test us on each RC** — what evidence, outside of case
    studies, do they want to see?
  - **Play the capabilities back and get explicit confirmation** before anyone
    builds on them.
- **Start with what the economic buyer is buying — never with our credentials.**
- **Learn the buyer's own vocabulary for their outcome, and use it back.**
  Hospitals talk about beds; apartment buildings talk about doors.
- **Defensible Differentiators** — uniquely/comparatively/holistically better,
  each tied to customer value and defensibility.
- **MEDDPICC** — qualification backbone. **Information is not influence.**
  *Knowing* the decision criteria and *having shaped* them are scored differently.
- **Trade access for access.** Offer senior Wpromote talent in exchange for access
  to the economic buyer and technical buyers.
- **If we can't tell people what to buy, in what order, and when, they'll buy the
  easiest thing — and the easiest thing is always more media.**
- **Discovery over being "pitched at."** Get discovery before you present.
- **Design discovery around the PBO and RCs, and prioritize hard.** Top 2 always:
  the economic buyer's PBO and technical buyers' view of the RCs. With limited
  questions, keep the Priority 1 list to ~5.
- **A PBO has to be measurable, or it isn't a PBO.** "Growth" is a direction.
- **The Economic Buyer needs three things: how much of the outcome, how soon, how
  sure.** A **Metric** is *the evidence a buyer uses to compare two vendors* on a
  Required Capability — not a number we like reporting.

**Discovery is where he'd spend the coaching hour.** *Don't answer — ask what it
costs them.* Before responding to anything a buyer raises, say "tell me more" or
say their concern back; then ask what problems that causes them. His read: the
team knows the right thing to say, but says it too early because they skipped a
basic follow-up question.

- **A bad discovery question asks them to be Google for you.** Ask one good
  question, let them talk ten minutes, and the remaining fifty are better.
- **Underpromise on expertise, overdeliver on preparation.**
- **Give a specialist rules of engagement before discovery.** *Should we pursue
  this* and *how would we win this* clear before solution-specific questions. He
  owns breaking this himself — letting a technical leader open, watching it go deep
  for fifteen minutes, and by the time he could ask what the buyer was solving for,
  they had no appetite to go back.
- **Ask for the pre-discovery one-on-one.**
- **Even the slide about us is a slide about them.**
- **Two-step for any insights/measurement conversation:** what decisions do you
  want insights to support that you can't make now, and what problems does lacking
  them cause?
